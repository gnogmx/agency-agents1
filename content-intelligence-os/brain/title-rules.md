# Title Rules

System-wide rules for titles. Titles are written and scored at idea stage, before scripting.

## The Job of a Title

A title converts an impression into a click by creating an information gap the viewer must close. It works WITH the thumbnail as one combined message: the thumbnail provokes ("what is this?"), the title frames ("here's the question this answers").

## Hard Rules

1. **Length: 50 characters or fewer preferred, 60 absolute max.** Titles truncate on mobile; the curiosity payload must survive truncation. Front-load the hook words.
2. **No title without a curiosity gap.** "The History of Nokia" states a topic. "The Meeting That Killed Nokia" opens a gap. Topic-statement titles are rejected on sight.
3. **Specificity signals substance.** Numbers, dates, dollar amounts, named entities. "How Kodak Lost $30 Billion" beats "How Kodak Lost Everything" — unless "Everything" is literally true, in which case absolutes win.
4. **The title must be honest.** Every factual claim in the title must be defensible from the video. Curiosity gap ≠ deception. Deception spikes CTR once and craters retention and trust forever.
5. **One title, one promise.** Titles that promise two things ("The Rise and Fall and Comeback of...") dilute the click. Pick the strongest single promise.
6. **Plain words beat clever words.** The viewer spends under a second parsing. Wordplay, puns, and irony routinely lose to blunt clarity. Save the wit for the script.
7. **No echo of the thumbnail text.** The pair must carry more combined information than either alone.

## Proven Structural Patterns (use as starting frames, not fill-in-blanks)

- **The Fatal Decision**: "The [Decision/Meeting/Deal] That Destroyed [Giant]"
- **The Paradox**: "Why [Company] Died at Its Peak" / "[Company] Won Every Battle and Lost the War"
- **The Hidden Cause**: "The Real Reason [Known Event] Happened"
- **The Scale Reveal**: "How [Underdog] Quietly Took Over [Industry]"
- **The Countdown**: "[Giant] Has [Timeframe] Left" (only with genuine evidence)
- **The Law**: "The One Rule [Company] Broke" (natural fit for lesson-driven brands)
- **The Money Trail**: "Who Actually Makes Money From [Familiar Thing]"

## Anti-Patterns (auto-reject)

- Listicle framing for documentary content ("5 Reasons Nokia Failed") — it fragments the narrative promise.
- ALL-CAPS words beyond one, or any clickbait punctuation ("?!", "...").
- "You Won't Believe" and other trust-burning phrasings.
- Inside-baseball jargon in the first 30 characters.
- Questions the viewer can answer without watching ("Did Nokia Fail?" — yes, next).
- Dates that make evergreen content feel stale ("...in 2024") unless recency IS the hook.

## Process Requirements

1. Minimum **7 candidate titles per idea**, spanning at least 3 different structural patterns.
2. Each scored against the checklist in `agents/04-viral-predictor.md` (gap strength, clarity at a glance, honesty, brand fit, competitive differentiation).
3. Top title is checked against actual current search results and suggested feeds: if an existing strong video uses a near-identical title, differentiate or reconsider.
4. All published titles and their CTR outcomes are logged in `memory/title-history.md`. Every 10 videos, the Learning Brain reviews which patterns are earning clicks FOR US — pattern performance is channel-specific and drifts over time.

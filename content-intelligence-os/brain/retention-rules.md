# Retention Rules

Rules for keeping viewers watching. Retention is the metric YouTube trusts most and the one small channels can compete on immediately — a 100-subscriber channel can out-retain a 10-million-subscriber channel.

## Retention Benchmarks (long-form documentary, 12–20 min)

- **First 30 seconds**: retain ≥ 70% of starters. Below 60% = hook failure or packaging mismatch.
- **First 2 minutes**: retain ≥ 55%. This is where the video proves the click was justified.
- **Average view duration (AVD)**: ≥ 40% is workable, ≥ 50% is strong, ≥ 60% is exceptional and should trigger deep pattern analysis of what we did.
- **End-of-video**: ≥ 20% reaching the final 30 seconds means the ending can actually drive session behavior.

These are analysis thresholds, not guarantees of distribution.

## The First 30 Seconds (highest-leverage zone)

1. **Deliver the thumbnail/title promise immediately or visibly begin delivering it.** The #1 cause of 30-second cliffs is packaging that wrote a check the opening doesn't start cashing.
2. **No intros.** No logo animations, no channel branding sequences, no "welcome back." Cold open, always.
3. **Confirm, then complicate.** First sentence confirms the viewer clicked the right video. Second move complicates it ("...but the collapse didn't start where everyone thinks").
4. **Visual change within the first 10 seconds.** A static opening shot reads as low effort before a single sentence lands.

## Mid-Video Retention Mechanics

1. **Pattern interrupt every 30–60 seconds**: cut, zoom, chart, archival footage, music shift, narration pace change. Documentary ≠ monotone.
2. **Re-hook every 2–3 minutes**: a mini-cliffhanger, a forward reference, a reframing fact. Map these in the script — they are engineered, not hoped for.
3. **Signpost progress.** Viewers stay when they sense structure: "That was the first crack. The second one came from inside the boardroom."
4. **Never front-load all context.** Background information is a retention tax — pay it in small installments exactly when the story needs each piece, not as a lump sum in minutes 2–5.
5. **Payoff spacing**: every open loop must close, and the biggest promised payoff must land past the 60% mark to pull viewers through the middle.

## Reading Retention Graphs (analysis rules)

- **Cliff in first 30s** → hook problem or packaging mismatch. Fix in packaging/hook design, not mid-video.
- **Steady linear decline** → normal. Compare slope against our channel median, not against zero.
- **Sudden mid-video cliff** → a specific failure: tangent, over-explained context block, energy drop, or a broken promise. Identify the exact timestamp and name the cause in `memory/`.
- **Bumps/spikes** → rewatched or skipped-to moments = the content viewers actually wanted. Spikes are a topic-selection signal: make videos about what people skip TO.
- **High retention + low views** → packaging failure, content success. The idea deserves a re-title/re-thumbnail and the topic deserves a second video.
- **Low retention + high CTR** → packaging overpromised. Trust damage; correct the next video's promise calibration.

## Structural Bans

- No "stay until the end" begging. Earn it structurally.
- No mid-video subscribe pitches before the midpoint payoff; one brief ask maximum, placed after a payoff moment, under 8 seconds.
- No sponsor reads inside the first 25% of a video (when sponsors exist) — early reads amputate the retention curve at its most valuable point.

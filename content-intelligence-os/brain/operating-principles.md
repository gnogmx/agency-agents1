# Operating Principles

These principles bind every agent and workflow in the system. When an agent's output conflicts with a principle, the output is rejected.

## 1. Decision Before Production

No script is written until an idea has passed research, scoring, and packaging validation. The order is fixed: idea → evidence → score → title/thumbnail concept → THEN script. A great script for an idea nobody clicks is a wasted script.

## 2. Packaging Is the Product

On YouTube, the title and thumbnail ARE the product at the moment of decision. The video is the fulfillment of a promise the packaging made. Therefore:
- Title and thumbnail concepts are created and scored BEFORE the script.
- If we cannot write a compelling title for an idea, the idea is not ready — regardless of how interesting the story is to us.

## 3. Evidence Beats Taste

Every claim an agent makes must cite evidence: a competitor video's actual performance, a search-volume observation, a pattern from our own `memory/` files. "I think this will work" is not an input to a decision. "Three channels under 100k subs got outlier views on this topic in the last 90 days" is.

## 4. Outlier Analysis, Not Average Analysis

We study videos that OVERPERFORMED their channel's baseline (view count ≥ 3× the channel's median). A big channel's average video succeeds because of the channel, not the idea. An outlier on a small channel succeeds because of the idea and packaging — that is the transferable signal.

## 5. Small Channel Reality

We assume zero algorithmic favor. Rules that only work with an existing audience (community posts, "as I said last video", multi-part series before video 30) are banned early. Every video must work as the first video a stranger ever sees from us.

## 6. One Variable Per Lesson

When analyzing why a video won or lost, isolate variables. A video with a new topic type AND a new thumbnail style AND a new hook structure teaches nothing when it fails. Workflows should change one major variable at a time when possible, and analysis must state which variable it credits.

## 7. Kill Fast, Document Always

An idea that scores below threshold dies at the research stage — no sunk-cost rescue. But every kill is logged with a reason so patterns of bad ideas become visible in `memory/failed-patterns.md`.

## 8. Memory Is Mandatory

An agent that produces output without reading the relevant `memory/` files first is malfunctioning. A workflow that ends without writing to `memory/` is incomplete. The system's only durable advantage is that it learns and individual videos do not.

## 9. Honest Uncertainty

Scores are probability estimates, not promises. Agents must express confidence levels and state what evidence is missing. A confident wrong prediction that goes unexamined poisons the memory files.

## 10. Human Holds the Green Light

The system recommends; a human approves production. The system's job is to make that approval decision take 10 minutes instead of 10 hours, with 10× better information.

# Content Strategy

How this system chooses what to make, in what order, and how strategy shifts as a channel matures.

## The Three-Filter Model

Every idea must pass three filters, in order:

1. **Demand filter** — Is there proven audience appetite? Evidence: outlier videos on comparable channels, search interest, active discussion (Reddit, X, news cycles). No demand evidence → dead.
2. **Winnability filter** — Can WE win this click against what already exists? If the top results are recent, high-production videos from big channels with the same angle, we lose by default. We need a differentiated angle, a fresher event, or an underserved sub-audience.
3. **Brand filter** — Does it compound the brand? A video can be clickable and winnable but off-brand (see `brands/<brand>/forbidden-patterns.md`). Off-brand wins train the algorithm to send us the wrong audience, which poisons future recommendations.

## Portfolio Allocation (per 10 videos)

New channels cannot afford pure experimentation or pure safety. Allocate production slots:

- **6 slots — Proven formats**: topics/formats that have already overperformed for us or for close competitors. These build the baseline.
- **3 slots — Adjacent bets**: same format, new topic territory; or same topic territory, new format element. One variable changed.
- **1 slot — Wild bet**: a genuine experiment. Documented as such in advance so a failure doesn't contaminate conclusions about the proven formats.

## Topic Selection Hierarchy (strongest to weakest signal)

1. A topic where 2+ small/mid channels produced 3×+ outliers in the last 6 months, and no dominant recent video exists in our specific angle.
2. A topic with a live news hook (bankruptcy filing, lawsuit, acquisition, scandal) that our format can cover with more depth than news channels.
3. A topic with persistent evergreen search demand and stale incumbent videos (2+ years old, weaker packaging standards).
4. A topic that performed for us before, approached from a new angle.
5. A topic we merely find interesting. (Weakest — requires exceptional packaging evidence to pass.)

## Cadence Strategy

- Consistency beats volume. One strong video per week beats three rushed ones — retention damage from a rushed video outlasts the extra impressions.
- Never let cadence pressure push an unscored idea into production. A skipped week costs less than a published flop that drags channel-level watch metrics.

## Cold-Start Sequence (videos 1–10)

1. Videos 1–3: our 3 highest-scoring proven-format ideas. These define what the algorithm thinks the channel is. Do not experiment here.
2. Videos 4–6: iterate on whichever of the first three had the best retention curve (not the best views — views are noise at this stage; retention is signal).
3. Videos 7–10: begin the 6/3/1 portfolio allocation.

## Strategy Review Trigger

Re-read and revise this file after every 10 published videos, using `memory/video-performance.md` and `memory/channel-lessons.md`. Strategy that ignores its own results is superstition.

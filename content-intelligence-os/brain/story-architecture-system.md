# Story Architecture System

The repeatable structural system the Story Architect (`agents/05-story-architect.md`) applies to every 12–18 minute Empire Laws episode. This is the blueprint standard: it defines the nine-section structure, the fields each section must carry, and the retention mechanics that make the structure work. It governs architecture only — no narration, no production code. Its single purpose is **retention**: keeping a cold, distractible stranger watching a business documentary to the Law Card and beyond.

## The Core Doctrine

1. **Curiosity order, not chronological order.** The true timeline is raw material. The *telling* is sequenced to open and close questions, not to march from founding to bankruptcy. A blueprint organized by year is a failed blueprint.
2. **Tension before information.** Every section earns the right to explain by first creating a reason to care. Background is a tax the viewer only pays after they're hooked.
3. **The company is the protagonist; the enemy is the weather.** The external disruptor (a rival, a technology, a market shift) is the force that exposes the company's internal flaw. It is never the main character. The story is what happened *inside* the empire.
4. **They were not stupid.** The most retentive and most honest Empire Laws frame is "they saw it and still fell." Contempt kills the tension — if the company was just dumb, there's no mystery and nothing transferable. The interesting question is always *why smart people with the right information couldn't act.*
5. **One contradiction runs the whole episode.** A single core contradiction (e.g., "they had the future first and lost to it") is the spine. Every section either deepens it or pays it off.
6. **No biography.** We architect around a mechanism and a law, not a person's life. Founders appear only as decision-makers at specific beats.

## The Nine-Section Structure

Fixed durations. The whole episode lands at 12–18 minutes (this template runs ~16:30 plus the final line).

| # | Section | Time | Structural job |
|---|---------|------|----------------|
| 1 | **Cold Open** | 0:00–0:45 | Immediate tension. Open on a contradiction, never on background. |
| 2 | **The Empire at the Top** | 0:45–2:30 | Show the power SYSTEM that made them dominant — and plant the seed of its weakness. |
| 3 | **The First Crack** | 2:30–4:30 | The first sign of vulnerability. Must contain a specific "they saw it, but…" moment. |
| 4 | **The Enemy Changes the Game** | 4:30–7:00 | The external shift. Show why the old rules — the ones the empire mastered — stopped working. |
| 5 | **The Internal Failure** | 7:00–10:30 | The REAL reason they couldn't respond: incentives, culture, speed, fear, debt, bureaucracy, or strategic blindness. The heart of the episode. |
| 6 | **The Collapse Point** | 10:30–13:00 | The irreversible decision or missed window. Name the exact moment the law was broken. |
| 7 | **The Empire Law** | 13:00–15:00 | Extract the transferable lesson. The Law Card moment. |
| 8 | **Modern Warning** | 15:00–16:30 | Connect the law to companies today. The viewer must think "this could happen again." |
| 9 | **Final Line** | final 10–20s | One memorable closing sentence. |

### How it maps to the brand format
This nine-section template is the detailed operating version of the arc in `brands/empire-laws/video-format.md`. Sections 1–2 = Cold Open + Stakes. Section 3 = the Fatal Seed. Sections 4–5 = the Turn (external trigger → internal cause). Section 6 = the Climax. Section 7 = the Law Card. Sections 8–9 = the Bridge. The two files must never contradict; if they drift, the video-format arc is the source of truth and this template is re-synced at a cohort review.

## Required Fields Per Section

Every section in a blueprint MUST carry all seven:

1. **Purpose** — the structural job this section does for the episode.
2. **Viewer question** — the single question in the viewer's mind entering (or created during) the section. This is what keeps them watching.
3. **Key beat** — the one concrete moment, fact, or reveal the section is built around.
4. **Visual metaphor** — the dominant visual idea (feeds the Design Director; not final code).
5. **Suggested on-screen text** — the short line(s) that may appear on screen (≤ ~6 words, per the premium visual system).
6. **Retention device** — the specific mechanism holding the viewer through this section (open loop, dramatic irony, reframe, payoff, pattern interrupt, identity hook).
7. **Transition into next section** — how the section hands off, ideally opening the next loop so there is no "flat" moment between sections.

## Retention Mechanics (the toolkit sections draw from)

- **Open loop:** plant a question early, pay it off later. The Cold Open opens the biggest loop; it closes at the Collapse Point.
- **Dramatic irony:** the audience knows what's coming (the iPhone, the bankruptcy) while the characters don't. Powerful in sections 2–4.
- **The reframe / turn:** a fact that flips the viewer's understanding of everything so far. Section 4 (rules changed) and section 5 (the real cause) are reframes.
- **The planted seed → payoff:** the weakness shown as a *strength* in section 2 becomes the *cause* in section 5. This is the single most important through-line.
- **Pattern interrupt:** a visual/mode change every 30–60 seconds so no section feels static (per `brain/retention-rules.md`).
- **Identity hook:** in section 8, the viewer applies the law to their own company — "is this us?" — which is what makes the lesson stick and the video shareable.

## Loop Accounting (mandatory)

A blueprint must explicitly list:
- **Open loops:** every question the structure plants, with the section where it opens.
- **Payoff moments:** the section where each open loop closes. No loop may be left open at the end.
- **The main question** (the episode's central mystery) opens in the Cold Open and pays off no later than the Collapse Point. If it pays off earlier, the middle sags; if later, the climax has no answer.

## How To Avoid Boring Biography (enforced check)

The failure mode this system exists to prevent. A blueprint passes only if:
- The Cold Open contains zero founding history.
- Section 2 explains a *power system* (how they made money and held dominance), not a chronology of milestones.
- No section is organized as "then in [year], the company did X, then in [year+1]…"
- Founders/executives appear only at decision beats, never as life stories.
- The through-line is a mechanism and a law, testable by this question: *if you removed the dates, would the story still make sense as a strategic argument?* If no, it's a biography — rebuild it.

## What Good Looks Like vs. What Fails

| Dimension | Strong architecture | Failed architecture |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Cold Open | A contradiction that demands resolution | "Nokia was founded in 1865 as a paper mill." |
| Structure | Ordered by curiosity; seeds and payoffs | Ordered by year; events listed |
| Enemy | A catalyst that exposes the internal flaw | The competitor's highlight reel |
| Cause | An internal, non-obvious reason (fear, speed, debt) | "They didn't innovate" / "they were slow" with no mechanism |
| Law | Specific, strategic, transferable | A generic moral ("adapt or die") |
| Ending | A warning that implicates the viewer's world | A summary of what happened |

## Calibration

Structure is tested by the retention curve. At each post-publish analysis, the Learning Brain maps cliffs and spikes to the section boundaries in the blueprint. Sections that repeatedly cause drop-off get their durations, devices, or order revised. This template is an instrument tuned by evidence, reviewed every 10 videos — not a fixed formula.

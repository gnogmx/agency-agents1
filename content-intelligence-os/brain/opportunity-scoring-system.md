# Opportunity Scoring System

The 100-point rubric the Viral Predictor (`agents/04-viral-predictor.md`) applies to every idea before production. This file defines exactly what earns each point and what loses it, so scoring is consistent across ideas, across weeks, and across whoever runs the agent.

**What this system is for:** helping a new channel escape the low-view trap by choosing better topics, better angles, better titles, and better thumbnails BEFORE production — when a bad decision costs minutes, not weeks. It scores decision quality. It does not predict virality and cannot guarantee views. A 90 is a strong bet, not a promise.

**Be strict.** The default posture is skepticism. Points are earned, not granted. When unsure between two scores, take the lower one. An inflated scorecard is worse than no scorecard, because it launches a doomed production with false confidence.

---

## The Eight Dimensions

### 1. Curiosity Gap — 20 points (the heaviest)

Does the idea open a specific, unanswered question a stranger scrolling the feed needs resolved? The gap is what earns the click. Everything else keeps them watching; this is what gets them in.

| Points | What it looks like |
|--------|--------------------|
| 17–20 | A sharp, specific gap that names an outcome without explaining it. "Nokia heard the future and ignored it" — WHAT did they hear? WHEN? You need to know. |
| 12–16 | A real gap, but broad or partly self-answering. "How Amazon won" — the gap exists but is diffuse. |
| 7–11 | Weak gap. The title mostly states a topic. "The history of Starbucks." |
| 0–6 | No gap. The viewer can answer the question from the thumbnail, or there is no question at all. |

**Losing points:** the answer is obvious ("Did WeWork fail?" — yes); the gap is generic and could be any company; the "gap" is actually just a topic label.

### 2. Market Proof — 15 points

Is there evidence that similar topics have earned views for comparable channels — ideally small ones (where an outlier proves the IDEA, not the channel)?

| Points | What it looks like |
|--------|--------------------|
| 13–15 | Multiple recent outliers (≥3× channel median) on comparable-size channels covering this topic or company. Proven, live demand. |
| 9–12 | Proven demand but on large channels, or somewhat dated, or the topic is saturated (proof cuts both ways — demand is real but so is competition). |
| 4–8 | Thin evidence: one weak signal, adjacent-topic proof only, or an old news cycle. |
| 0–3 | No evidence anyone wants this. A guess dressed as an opportunity. |

**Note:** high Market Proof on a saturated topic is a flag, not a green light. Proof means demand exists; it does not mean WE can win it. Winnability is judged in the Novel Angle score and the Gap Finder's brief.

### 3. Novel Angle — 15 points

Is the angle different from the generic company documentary that already exists on this topic? Empire Laws does not tell company stories — it extracts a transferable law. The angle is where that differentiation lives.

| Points | What it looks like |
|--------|--------------------|
| 13–15 | A frame nobody else uses: a strategic mechanism, a single fatal decision, a hidden controller. "Starbucks is a habit machine" reframes coffee as behavioral engineering. |
| 9–12 | A recognizable frame executed with a specific twist. "Toys R Us killed by its own buyout" is a known genre with a sharp mechanism. |
| 4–8 | Generic framing. "The rise and fall of X." Owned by bigger channels; we lose by default. |
| 0–3 | We would be the tenth identical video, later and smaller than the incumbents. |

**Losing points:** any angle a viewer has already seen from a Tier 1 channel; "biography of the founder"; angles that only work because nobody big has bothered — usually a sign of no demand.

### 4. Emotional Tension — 15 points

Does the story carry real dramatic charge — conflict, betrayal, collapse, ambition, arrogance, risk, survival? Tension is retention fuel. A strategy lesson with no drama is a lecture, and lectures lose the feed.

| Points | What it looks like |
|--------|--------------------|
| 13–15 | Multiple charged elements with a clear arc: hubris → collapse, betrayal → war, desperation → survival. WeWork (arrogance + delusion + collapse), Nokia (tragic hubris). |
| 9–12 | One strong element and a clear stake. Uber's chaos and scandal; Airbnb's near-death cereal survival. |
| 5–8 | Mild tension. A rise story with ambition but no conflict or fall — Disney's nostalgia machine, Starbucks' habit engine. Interesting, less gripping. |
| 0–4 | Flat. A process explainer with no human stakes. |

**Rule:** collapse and war stories start higher here than pure rise/mechanism stories. That is not bias — it reflects what the retention curve rewards. A rise story can still score well elsewhere, but it must earn tension some other way (a villain, a ticking clock, a near-death moment).

### 5. Audience Fit — 10 points

Does it land squarely with the Empire Laws audience: entrepreneurs, founders, creators, investors, business students, and business-documentary viewers?

| Points | What it looks like |
|--------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | Bullseye. A strategic business story with a transferable lesson this audience can use in their own decisions. |
| 6–8 | On-target but leans one segment (e.g., very investor-heavy, or very consumer-brand). |
| 3–5 | Adjacent. General-interest business trivia with a weak strategic takeaway. |
| 0–2 | Off-audience. Would pull the wrong viewers and confuse the algorithm's model of the channel. |

**Losing points:** stories that flatter no aspiration; topics that attract passive consumers rather than operators; anything that reads as general news rather than strategy.

### 6. Thumbnail Potential — 10 points

Can the idea be shown in ONE powerful image that passes the glance test (`brain/thumbnail-rules.md`) — legible at postage-stamp size, provoking a question in under a second?

| Points | What it looks like |
|--------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | An obvious, strong single image with built-in tension. Blockbuster store going dark vs. Netflix glow; a toy-store gravestone; a Nokia phone cracking. |
| 6–8 | A workable image that needs craft to land. Airbnb's air mattress vs. a skyline. |
| 3–5 | Abstract subject, hard to visualize. "Attention as a product," "habit machine" — concepts resist single images. |
| 0–2 | No visual hook. Nothing to show but a logo. |

**Losing points:** the core idea is a concept with no physical referent; the only image is a corporate logo; two competing focal points that can't coexist in one frame.

### 7. Title Potential — 10 points

Can the idea produce strong, clickable titles WITHOUT clickbait — a real curiosity gap, honest, specific, under ~50 characters (`brain/title-rules.md`)?

| Points | What it looks like |
|--------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | Multiple strong single-promise titles write themselves. "The Meeting That Killed Nokia." |
| 6–8 | Good titles available but tempted toward two promises or vagueness; needs discipline. |
| 3–5 | Titles come out either boring (topic labels) or dishonest (clickbait) — hard to hit the honest-gap sweet spot. |
| 0–2 | No clickable honest title exists for this framing. |

**Losing points:** the only compelling titles require deception; the story is too broad for a specific title; every honest title is a flat topic statement.

### 8. Production Feasibility — 5 points

Can we produce it with our actual toolkit — script, voiceover, Remotion/motion graphics, charts, public research — with NO expensive filming, no paid access, no rights we can't clear?

| Points | What it looks like |
|--------|--------------------|
| 5 | Fully feasible. Public research, chartable data, archival/stock imagery, motion graphics. The default for this format. |
| 3–4 | Feasible but asset-heavy: needs specific archival footage or complex data viz that takes real time. |
| 1–2 | Feasibility risk: depends on footage/access/rights we may not clear, or data that may not be public. |
| 0 | Not feasible with our tools without filming or paid access. |

**Note:** this dimension is worth only 5 points because for a research-and-motion-graphics documentary channel almost everything is feasible. Its job is to catch the rare idea that secretly requires something we don't have — before it derails production.

## Scoring Bands & Decisions

| Score | Decision | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **90–100** | Produce immediately | Commit the slot. Rare — expect these seldom. |
| **80–89** | Strong candidate | Fix the weakest dimension (usually title, hook, or angle), THEN produce. |
| **70–79** | Possible | Produce only if strategically important. Otherwise hold or drop. |
| **60–69** | Weak | Reposition — send back to the Gap Finder for a new angle. Do not produce this version. |
| **Below 60** | Reject | Say it plainly, log the reason, move on. No rescue. |

## Rules of Honest Scoring

1. **Round down when unsure.** The cost of a false 82 (a doomed production) dwarfs the cost of a false 78 (a slightly delayed good idea).
2. **Score against reality, not potential.** Score the idea and packaging as they exist now, not the perfect version in your head. If the title is weak now, Title Potential is weak now.
3. **Packaging is scored against the live feed.** Thumbnail and Title dimensions are judged beside the ACTUAL competing thumbnails and titles for the topic, not in isolation.
4. **No dimension is skippable.** Every idea gets all eight scores with evidence. A missing justification voids the scorecard.
5. **Saturation is a penalty, not a bonus.** High Market Proof on an over-covered topic must be checked by a Novel Angle score honest about the crowd.
6. **The weakest dimension is the headline.** An 84 with a 4/10 title is not an 84 you produce — it is an idea whose title you fix first. Report the weakest link loudly.
7. **Calibrate quarterly.** Every 10 videos, the Learning Brain checks whether high scores actually outperformed. If they didn't, these point definitions and weights get revised. This rubric is an instrument, and instruments drift.

## What This System Refuses to Do

- Promise that a high-scoring idea will get views. It won't promise that. It improves the odds and the decision; the feed decides.
- Rescue a topic the channel is emotionally attached to. Attachment is not a dimension.
- Treat "we already did the research" as a reason to produce. Sunk cost is not a score.
- Let one great dimension carry a broken idea. A perfect thumbnail cannot save a story with no curiosity gap and no tension.

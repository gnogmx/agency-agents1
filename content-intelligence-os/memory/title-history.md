# Title History — Empire Laws

Complete, uncurated log of every published title (and every post-publish title change). Written at publish by the Publisher, completed with outcomes by the Learning Brain at T+7d and T+28d. This is raw pattern-mining material — no editorializing, no deletions.

## Why Uncurated

Pattern mining needs the full distribution, including mediocre results. A curated "best titles" list can't reveal that, for example, our paradox-pattern titles cluster at 3–4% CTR while our fatal-decision titles cluster at 5–6% — the comparison IS the insight. Every 10 videos, the Learning Brain mines this file for the cohort review: which structural patterns (per `brain/title-rules.md`) are earning clicks FOR US, and how is that drifting over time.

## Log Format (one entry per published title)

```
### [video number]. "[exact published title]"
- Slug: [slug] | Published: YYYY-MM-DD | Pillar: [1–4]
- Structural pattern: [Fatal Decision / Paradox / Hidden Cause / Scale Reveal / Countdown / The Law / Money Trail / other-described]
- Character count: NN | Numbers/specifics used: [list]
- Runner-up titles considered: ["...", "..."] (from the scorecard's 7+ candidates)
- Predicted CTR band: X–Y% (frozen, from scorecard)
- Actual CTR: T+48h: __% (on __ impressions) | T+7d: __% | T+28d: __%
- vs. channel trailing median CTR at publish: [median value] → [above/below/at]
- Title changed post-publish? [no / yes: old → new, date, reason, before/after CTR]
- Learning Brain verdict: [one line — did the title do its job independent of the video?]
```

## Mining Questions (cohort review checklist)

1. Which structural patterns hold the highest median CTR for us? (Count ≥ 2 uses before trusting.)
2. Is character count correlating with CTR? (Watch the mobile truncation boundary, ~50 chars.)
3. Are specifics (dollar amounts, dates, named entities) earning their space?
4. Are any patterns fatiguing — declining CTR on repeated use?
5. Did any post-publish title swap produce a clear before/after delta? (Cleanest natural experiments we get.)

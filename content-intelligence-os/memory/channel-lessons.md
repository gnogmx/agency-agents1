## Standing Priors (pre-launch)

These are NOT lessons — they are starting assumptions imported from `brain/` and `brands/` research, held until our own data confirms, refines, or overturns them. Each is written falsifiably so the first videos can test them. When one is confirmed or overturned by our data, it is converted into a numbered lesson below with evidence.

### P-1: Collapse framings will out-click rise framings for a cold audience
- Basis: collapse stories dominate the outlier lists of Tier 1/2 business documentary channels.
- Test: compare CTR of Pillar 1 vs. Pillar 2 episodes at matched impression counts over the first 10 videos.

### P-2: The "single fatal decision" angle will out-retain broad-history angles
- Basis: platform-wide retention logic (one spine, one climax) per `brain/storytelling-rules.md`.
- Test: retention slope comparison between decision-framed and history-framed episodes, if we ever produce the latter.

### P-3: Famous-company stories will outperform obscure-company stories early
- Basis: cold audiences click recognition + curiosity; obscurity requires channel trust we don't yet have.
- Test: CTR comparison by subject familiarity tier across the first 10–20 videos. (If overturned — obscure "Hidden Empire" stories winning — Pillar 4 allocation rises.)

### P-4: The named Law will be a retention asset, not a liability, at the end of episodes
- Basis: the Law Card is our differentiator; end-segment retention will show whether viewers stay for it.
- Test: end-of-video retention (final 90s) vs. the point just before the Law Card across early episodes.

### P-5: 12–18 minutes is the right runtime band for our format at channel size zero
- Basis: long enough for depth and watch-time value, short enough to hold AVD % — per format research.
- Test: AVD% by runtime within the band; revisit the band at the 30-video mark.

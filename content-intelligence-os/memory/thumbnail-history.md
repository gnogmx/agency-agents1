# Thumbnail History — Empire Laws

Complete, uncurated log of every published thumbnail, every swap, and every outcome. Written at publish by the Thumbnail Lab/Publisher, completed by the Learning Brain at T+7d and T+28d. Together with `title-history.md`, this is how the channel discovers its OWN visual language instead of renting competitors' forever.

## Log Format (one entry per published thumbnail)

```
### [video number]. [slug]
- Published: YYYY-MM-DD | Paired title: "[exact title]" | Pillar: [1–4]
- Curiosity device: [unexplained anomaly / before-after collision / forbidden document / scale shock / moment before disaster / other-described]
- Composition: [subject] + [context signal] + [tension device] | Text: "[≤3 words or none]"
- Visual-system elements used: [subject treatment / gold fracture / chart ghost / none]
- Concepts it beat: [the other 2+ concepts from the idea-stage batch, one line each]
- Feed-test opponents at design time: [the 6 competitor thumbnails it was tested against]
- Predicted CTR band: X–Y% (frozen)
- Actual CTR: T+48h: __% (on __ impressions) | T+7d: __% | T+28d: __%
- Swap executed? [no / yes: date, trigger CTR, swap device, before/after CTR at matched impression volume]
- Learning Brain verdict: [one line — device performance independent of topic strength, as far as separable]
```

## Mining Questions (cohort review checklist)

1. Which devices earn above our trailing median CTR? Which underperform regardless of topic?
2. Are the brand elements (gold fracture, dark premium grade) helping or hurting glance-contrast in real feeds?
3. Do faces (with legible emotion) beat object/scene compositions for us, or not?
4. What do the swap experiments show at matched impressions? (Swaps are our cleanest A/B data.)
5. Are we accidentally converging on one device (portfolio risk: feed blindness through self-similarity)?

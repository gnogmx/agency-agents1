# Empire Laws — Competitor Map

The channels whose audiences, packaging standards, and suggested-feed placements define our competitive environment. This file is the standing input to the Competitor Brain agent (`agents/02-competitor-brain.md`), which keeps a live tracking sheet in `outputs/analytics/`.

## How to Use This Map

- **Tier 1** defines the quality bar and the suggested-feed inventory we want to appear beside.
- **Tier 2** is our primary outlier-mining ground: channels small enough that an outlier proves the IDEA, not the channel (operating principle 4).
- **Tier 3** shows adjacent audiences we may inherit traffic from.
- We study competitors for demand evidence and packaging patterns. We never copy a specific video's angle wholesale — if they made it well, the demand is now partly served (winnability filter).

## Tier 1 — Direct Format Leaders (business documentary, high production)

| Channel | What they own | What we learn | Where they're beatable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magnates Media | Cinematic long-form business stories, thriller pacing | Packaging standards, hook construction, story-first structure | Very long runtimes leave a 12–18 min "premium but complete" gap; no systematic takeaway |
| Modern MBA | Deep analytical business breakdowns | Depth expectations, analytical credibility | Slower pacing; analysis-first, weaker narrative drama |
| Company Man | Broad "rise/decline of X company" coverage | Proven topic demand list (his catalog = a demand map) | Simpler production; no cinematic treatment; no law/lesson layer |
| Wall Street Millennial | Business scandals and failures, fast turnaround | News-hook responsiveness | Utilitarian packaging; minimal narrative craft |
| How Money Works | Explainer-style money mechanisms | "Hidden system" topic demand | Explainer format, not documentary; no story arc to compete with |

## Tier 2 — Outlier-Mining Set (small/mid channels in our territory)

Maintained live by the Competitor Brain: channels under ~500k subs producing business story content, where any video at ≥ 3× channel median is a topic/packaging signal. Seed criteria for building the list:
- Business story/documentary format, active in the last 90 days
- At least one 3× outlier in the last 6 months
- Packaging quality low enough that our execution can win the same click

## Tier 3 — Adjacent Audience Sources

| Channel type | Examples | Overlap |
|---|---|---|
| Finance/econ explainers | Economics Explained, TLDR Business | Same viewer, different mode; their commenters ask for "the full story of X" |
| Tech video essays | ColdFusion | Rise/fall tech stories; premium tone reference |
| True-crime style business | Coffeezilla | Scandal appetite; investigation framing patterns |
| Aviation/industry disaster docs | Mentour Pilot class | Proof that premium niche documentary retains; structure reference |

## Standing Competitive Questions (Competitor Brain answers weekly)

1. Which topics produced Tier 2 outliers this week, and is the angle still unserved by Tier 1?
2. What packaging devices (thumbnail devices, title patterns) are rising across tiers?
3. Which Tier 1 uploads underperformed their channel median — and does that mark topic fatigue we should avoid?
4. Whose suggested feeds do our videos appear in (once live), and what does that say about how YouTube classifies us?

## Explicit Non-Competitors

Hustle-culture motivation channels, day-trading/stock-tip channels, AI-slop listicle "top 10 companies" channels. We do not study their packaging, chase their topics, or accept their audiences as targets — their viewer expectations poison our retention and brand (see `forbidden-patterns.md`).

# Empire Laws — Video Format

The canonical episode blueprint. **Every Empire Laws long-form video must use the 9-section structure defined below.** It is the single source of truth for episode structure across the system; the Story Architect (`agents/05-story-architect.md`) and the Story Architecture System (`brain/story-architecture-system.md`) build against it, and no episode is scripted until its structure is approved against this format. Deviations require an "adjacent bet" slot (see `brain/content-strategy.md`) and an explicit note on the idea scorecard.

## Specs

- **Length**: 12–18 minutes target. The 9-section template runs to ~16:30 plus the final line, landing in the upper half of the band. Long enough for documentary depth and watch-time value; short enough to hold a premium retention curve while the channel earns trust. Re-evaluate the band at 30 videos.
- **Narration**: single narrator, measured delivery (~150–160 wpm), close-mic, lower register. Script style per `brain/storytelling-rules.md`.
- **Visual base**: motion-graphics documentary — animated stills, archival footage, charts, maps, and typographic moments in the house visual system (`visual-system.md`). No talking head.
- **Music**: continuous score in movements matching the section arc; silence as a deliberate device before the Empire Law (Section 7).

## Canonical Episode Structure — The 9 Sections

Fixed order, fixed duration ranges. Every episode is built on these nine sections.

| # | Section | Duration | Must accomplish |
|---|---------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Cold Open** | 0:00–0:45 | Create immediate tension by opening on a contradiction; state or imply the central question. |
| 2 | **The Empire at the Top** | 0:45–2:30 | Show the power SYSTEM that made the company dominant, and plant the seed of its weakness inside that strength. |
| 3 | **The First Crack** | 2:30–4:30 | Reveal the first sign of vulnerability, including a specific "they saw it, but…" moment. |
| 4 | **The Enemy Changes the Game** | 4:30–7:00 | Introduce the external shift and show why the old rules — the ones the empire mastered — stopped working. |
| 5 | **The Internal Failure** | 7:00–10:30 | Deliver the REAL reason the company couldn't respond: incentives, culture, speed, fear, debt, bureaucracy, or strategic blindness. |
| 6 | **The Collapse Point** | 10:30–13:00 | Name the irreversible decision or missed window — the exact moment the Empire Law was broken. |
| 7 | **The Empire Law** | 13:00–15:00 | Extract the transferable lesson as the Law Card moment; make it usable to founders, operators, investors, and creators. |
| 8 | **Modern Warning** | 15:00–16:30 | Connect the law to companies today so the viewer thinks, "this could happen again." |
| 9 | **Final Line** | final 10–20s | Land one memorable closing sentence that seals the law. |

### Section-by-Section — What Each Must Accomplish and Must Not Do

**1. Cold Open (0:00–0:45)**
- Must: open on a contradiction or peak-tension image/fact; state or imply the main question; deliver the packaging promise's premise instantly.
- Must NOT: open with company founding, history, dates, branding, greetings, or "in this video." No background of any kind in the first 45 seconds.

**2. The Empire at the Top (0:45–2:30)**
- Must: explain the power *system* — how they made money and held dominance — with anchored numbers; plant the fatal seed (the strength that will become the weakness).
- Must NOT: become a chronological list of milestones or product launches; praise the company without planting the seed.

**3. The First Crack (2:30–4:30)**
- Must: show the first real vulnerability; include a concrete "they saw it, but…" moment; open a loop about why they couldn't act.
- Must NOT: jump straight to the external competitor; frame the company as blind or stupid.

**4. The Enemy Changes the Game (4:30–7:00)**
- Must: introduce the external shift as a change in the *rules*; show why the empire's strengths stopped mattering.
- Must NOT: turn into the competitor's highlight reel; let the external enemy become the protagonist of the episode.

**5. The Internal Failure (7:00–10:30)**
- Must: deliver the true internal cause (culture, speed, fear, debt, bureaucracy, incentives, blindness); pay off the "why couldn't they act" loops. This is the heart of the episode.
- Must NOT: settle for "a rival beat them" as the explanation; reduce the cause to a single villain executive; lecture without dramatizing.

**6. The Collapse Point (10:30–13:00)**
- Must: name the single irreversible decision or missed window; pay off the main question; state plainly where the law broke.
- Must NOT: leave the collapse diffuse ("things slowly declined"); introduce major new characters this late.

**7. The Empire Law (13:00–15:00)**
- Must: state the law in ≤ 7 words as the full-screen Law Card (`visual-system.md`); make it transferable in 2–3 sentences; turn the story toward the viewer's own world.
- Must NOT: deliver a generic moral ("adapt or die"); bolt on a law the story didn't earn; exceed 7 words on the card.

**8. Modern Warning (15:00–16:30)**
- Must: connect the law to present-day companies or industries; trigger the "is this us?" reflection.
- Must NOT: name companies we can't defend from documented evidence; drift into unrelated predictions; summarize the video.

**9. Final Line (final 10–20s)**
- Must: deliver one memorable closing sentence; ideally mirror the Cold Open image to bookend; hand off to the end screen.
- Must NOT: say "thanks for watching," beg for subscribes, or add a summary. End on momentum, not closure.

## Signature Elements (every episode)

1. **The Law Card** (Section 7) — the brand's contract with the viewer. The law, named and stated in ≤ 7 words. Never skipped, never moved before the Collapse Point.
2. **The Fatal Seed** — the weakness planted in Section 2 (The Empire at the Top) that pays off in Section 5 (The Internal Failure). Structures without a fatal seed are returned for revision.
3. **The "They Saw It, But…" Moment** — the specific beat in Section 3 that establishes the company was not stupid, only unable to act. The frame the whole channel depends on.
4. **One-Point Charts** — every data visual makes exactly one point (per `visual-system.md`).
5. **Anchored Numbers** — every large number gets a human-scale comparison.

## Subscribe / CTA Policy

One subscribe mention maximum, ≤ 8 seconds, placed directly after the Collapse Point payoff (Section 6), framed on brand value ("we extract one law from one empire, every week"). No like-begging, no notification-bell theater, no mid-roll self-promotion.

## Format Approval Checklist (before scripting)

A video's structure must pass every item below before a single line of narration is written. This is the gate at the end of the story-blueprint workflow (`workflows/story-blueprint-workflow.md`).

- [ ] The structure uses all 9 canonical sections, in order, with none merged or dropped
- [ ] Each section falls within its duration range and the total lands at 12–18 minutes
- [ ] Section 1 (Cold Open) opens on a contradiction — zero background, branding, or history in the first 45 seconds
- [ ] Section 2 explains a power SYSTEM and plants the fatal seed (not a milestone timeline)
- [ ] Section 3 contains a specific, concrete "they saw it, but…" moment
- [ ] Section 4 frames the external shift as a change in the rules, and does not let the enemy become the protagonist
- [ ] Section 5 delivers a real internal cause (culture / speed / fear / debt / bureaucracy / incentives / blindness), not "a rival appeared"
- [ ] Section 6 names the exact irreversible decision or missed window where the law broke
- [ ] Section 7 states the Empire Law in ≤ 7 words and makes it transferable to founders, operators, investors, and creators
- [ ] Section 8 connects the law to a present-day company or industry with defensible evidence
- [ ] Section 9 is one memorable closing line, with no summary or "thanks for watching"
- [ ] The main question opens in Section 1 and pays off no later than Section 6; every open loop has a designated payoff
- [ ] The company is framed as smart-but-unable-to-act, never as stupid
- [ ] The biography check passes: remove all dates and the structure still reads as a strategic argument
- [ ] Title/thumbnail promise is paid at or before the Collapse Point (Section 6)

## Deprecated — Historical Context Only (NOT an active format)

> The following 8-beat arc was the original Empire Laws format. **It is deprecated and must not be used for any new episode.** It is retained here only as historical context to explain earlier system files and the mapping below. All future episodes use the 9-section structure above.

The retired 8-beat arc was: Cold Open → The Stakes → Act 1 (The Build) → The Turn → Act 2 (The Consequence) → The Climax → The Law Card → The Bridge.

For reference, it maps onto the canonical 9-section structure as follows:

| Deprecated 8-beat arc | Canonical 9-section structure |
|---|---|
| Cold Open | 1. Cold Open |
| The Stakes | 2. The Empire at the Top |
| Act 1 — The Build (+ Fatal Seed) | 2–3. The Empire at the Top / The First Crack |
| The Turn | 4–5. The Enemy Changes the Game / The Internal Failure |
| Act 2 — The Consequence | 5. The Internal Failure |
| The Climax | 6. The Collapse Point |
| The Law Card | 7. The Empire Law |
| The Bridge | 8–9. Modern Warning / Final Line |

If any other file still references the 8-beat arc as the current format, it is out of date and must be re-synced to the 9-section structure at the next cohort review.

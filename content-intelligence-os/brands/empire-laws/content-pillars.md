# Empire Laws — Content Pillars

The topic territories Empire Laws operates in. Every idea must belong to exactly one pillar. Pillars concentrate the channel's identity so the algorithm and audience learn what a click delivers (growth rule R10).

## Pillar 1 — The Collapse (primary pillar, ~40% of slots)

How a dominant company died or nearly died, and the law it broke.

- Shape: peak → hidden flaw → unraveling → collapse → law.
- Why it leads: collapse stories are the proven highest-demand format in business documentary; the audience inventory already exists (growth rule R3).
- Example episodes: "The Meeting That Killed Nokia" (law: platforms win by owning demand), "How Toys 'R' Us Was Killed by Its Own Debt" (law: debt kills flexibility), "Kodak Invented Its Own Killer" (law: protecting the past forfeits the future).

## Pillar 2 — The Rise (~25% of slots)

How an empire was actually built — the specific decision, deal, or mechanism behind a familiar giant, and the law it proves.

- Shape: unlikely origin → the pivotal mechanism → domination → law.
- Angle discipline: never a generic founder biography (see forbidden patterns). The episode is about a MECHANISM (a contract, a channel, a pricing move), not a person's life story.
- Example episodes: "The Contract That Built Microsoft" (law: distribution beats product), "How LVMH Manufactures Desire" (law: luxury is perception control).

## Pillar 3 — The War (~20% of slots)

Two empires in direct conflict — the battle, the decisive move, the law that determined the winner.

- Shape: two protagonists → collision course → the decisive asymmetry → verdict → law.
- Strength: built-in conflict structure = strongest natural retention mechanics of any pillar.
- Example episodes: "Netflix vs. Blockbuster: The $50 Million Insult" (law: incumbents defend margins, insurgents attack habits), "Airbus vs. Boeing: How the Student Beat the Master."

## Pillar 4 — The Hidden Empire (~15% of slots)

A company or force that dominates a familiar part of life while staying invisible — who actually controls X, who really profits from Y.

- Shape: familiar surface → hidden controller reveal → how the machine works → law.
- Strength: highest curiosity-gap potential for packaging; strongest "insider knowledge" identity payoff.
- Example episodes: "The Company That Controls What You Eat" (Cargill), "Who Actually Makes Money From Your iPhone" (law: the choke point captures the profit).

## Pillar Rules

1. Every idea is tagged with its pillar in `outputs/ideas/` scorecards; pillar performance is compared at every 10-video cohort review, and allocation percentages are revised based on `memory/video-performance.md` evidence.
2. A story that fits two pillars is framed as the pillar with the stronger packaging evidence — the Gap Finder and Viral Predictor decide, not preference.
3. New pillars may be proposed only at cohort reviews, only with outlier evidence from competitors, and enter through "adjacent bet" slots (see `brain/content-strategy.md`).
4. Whatever the pillar, the episode delivers the brand contract: one story, one named law, one Law Card moment.
